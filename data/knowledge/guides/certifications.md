# Certifications and transport

## UN38.3
A set of eight transport tests: altitude simulation, thermal, vibration, shock, external short circuit, impact or crush, overcharge and forced discharge. Lithium cells and batteries must pass before they can be shipped. It proves the pack survives transport; it is not a full product safety certification.

## IEC 62133-2
Safety requirements for portable sealed secondary lithium cells and batteries in portable applications. A common baseline for e-bike, tool and consumer packs.

## IEC 62619
Safety requirements for secondary lithium cells and batteries in industrial applications, including stationary storage.

## UL standards
UL 2054 covers household and commercial batteries, UL 1642 covers lithium cells, and UL 2271 covers batteries for light electric vehicles. They are North American standards, but suppliers often cite them.

## Australia
Fixed battery systems: AS/NZS 5139. Electrical products supplied in Australia may also need to meet electrical safety and RCM (Regulatory Compliance Mark) requirements, which vary by product and state. Check current rules with the relevant regulator before selling or importing.

## Shipping and flying
- Lithium-ion batteries shipped alone are UN3480; packed with or contained in equipment they are UN3481.
- Road transport in Australia follows the Australian Dangerous Goods Code. Air freight follows the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, which require standalone lithium-ion batteries to be at or below 30% state of charge.
- Passenger carry-on limits: 100 Wh or less is generally allowed; 100-160 Wh needs airline approval; over 160 Wh is not allowed on passenger aircraft. Spare batteries go in carry-on, not checked baggage.
